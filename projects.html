Case Study: The Expo Window as an Information System

When people hear "information system," they usually picture computers and software. Working as an expeditor at JOEY Bellevue has shown me that an information system is really any process that collects, organizes, and delivers information so people can act on it. During a busy dinner service, the expo window is exactly that kind of system.

The Inputs

Every order begins as data. A server enters it into the point-of-sale system, and that information is routed to the right kitchen stations as tickets. Special requests, allergies, and timing notes all travel with the order. If any piece of that data is missing or unclear, the final plate will be wrong.

The Process

As the expeditor, I act as the checkpoint where information and product come together. I track which tickets are in progress, call for dishes so a table's food finishes at the same time, and confirm each plate matches the order. In MIS terms, I am performing quality control and coordination between two departments: the kitchen and the front of house.

The Lessons

This role taught me that good systems depend on clear communication, accurate data, and people who understand both sides of a process. It also showed me how small delays or errors can ripple through an entire operation. These lessons shape how I approach my coursework: technology is most valuable when it makes everyday work simpler and more reliable for the people doing it.
